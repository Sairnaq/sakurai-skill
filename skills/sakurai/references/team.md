# Team management (decisions, information sharing, testing, roles)

Summaries based on the Japanese captions for Masahiro Sakurai on Creating Games. Episode numbers follow the repository’s oldest-first source order.

## 043 Sharing Info Within a Team [Team Management]
- As teams grow, it becomes harder to see what everyone is building and why. Share progress so people understand how their work contributes.
- Use an internal development page with comments, a daily development screenshot, and occasional presentations where each group shares its results.
- Presentations take preparation and give everyone a view of the project. They can break up routine and make progress visible.

## 050 Jobs in Game Development [Team Management]
- The director pitches, decides, and supervises. Designers turn ideas into specifications and may handle balance, testing direction, and scripts. Programmers implement the specifications and integrate assets.
- Artists can specialize in models, 2D art, animation and rigs, effects, or UI. Animators and effects artists make movement and feedback suit the game.
- Other roles cover audio, production tools, schedules, staffing, purchasing, playtesting, bug reproduction, and gameplay capture. Designers turn playtester feedback into tuning changes.
- Share goals and completion criteria so the roles can work together.

## 060 Don't Wait to Speak Your Mind [Team Management]
- Raise concerns about the design or how the team works when you notice them. Feedback after completion comes too late to use.
- Team members can help fix the problems they spot. Give the team a chance to hear about them.
- Pair criticism with a possible fix. Explain the decision if the current design stays.
- Use an open suggestion channel and respond to the ideas the team considers.
- Listen with an open mind and decide promptly whether to act.

## 088 Ten People Can Produce Seven People's Work [Team Management]
- A ten-person team has roughly seven people’s worth of hands-on production time once management, coordination, external communication, and supervision are accounted for.
- A skilled person who takes a lead role produces less direct work. Larger teams also spend more time coordinating, including around tool and security rules.
- Ask groups to share results periodically. It gives everyone the broader view and helps staff practice presenting their work.

## 122 Daily Report Suggestions [Team Management]
- Share a daily screenshot and three to five short topics, such as news, items to check, bugs, impressions, or trivia. Let staff comment.
- Reports help staff get to know an incoming director, put emailed information in one place, build a habit of writing, add variety, and show progress beyond each person’s task.
- A place for staff updates and suggestions can help the team communicate.

## 135 Bug Testing Never Ends [Team Management]
- Characters, moves, items, terrain, and additional actors create too many combinations for exhaustive testing.
- Target likely trouble spots. Test grabs and throws that force another actor to move, scene changes that can leave artifacts behind, unusual terrain, and parameter and progression interactions.
- A fix can create another bug. Give the team time to test affected cases before releasing a patch.
- Set a realistic quality bar so the game can ship.

## 160 Making Games as Part of a Company [Team Management]
- A company can provide a regular salary, space and equipment, training, tool research, licensing, and translation. It can spread risk and let people specialize.
- Sales can be polarized. When a title underperforms, factors such as packaging, title, advertising, timing, and competition make any single explanation hard to prove.
- Working with others creates friction and offers mutual support. Give staff room to take ownership.

## 175 Finish Everything Within the Day [Team Management]
- Sakurai handles review requests in the order they arrive by email. Send decisions and corrections to everyone affected.
- Reply to incoming requests on the same day where possible. Delaying the response can block another person’s work and make the next day busier.
- Reserve blocks for your own work. Sakurai used mornings for work that did not involve supervision.

## 189 Don’t Put Decisions Off [Team Management]
- When someone with decision authority is present, aim to resolve the question in the meeting.
- A decision left pending can hold up everyone downstream. The director’s job includes setting the course quickly.
- Ask staff to bring accurate information and research so the team can make a sound decision on the spot.
- Make another decision when you find a mistake. Small course corrections can happen as often as needed.

## 206 Bug Testing Systems [Team Management]
- Agree on the daily ROM build so bugs can be tested against the same version.
- Full-time testers combine cases from the specification and divide coverage by area using checklists.
- Log reproducible steps in a ticket and hand it to an owner so the report is tracked.
- Leads prioritize issues, such as hangs or save corruption at S and blocked progress at A, and direct them to the responsible discipline. Test the fix with related combinations, keep the closed report for future reference, and link duplicate or related issues.
- Keep workloads visible and redistribute tickets when someone has too many.
- Use overnight automated play for large, varied games to catch unusual cases. Late in production, teams in different regions can hand off testing across time zones.
- Set up short feedback loops so fixes that introduce new bugs can be caught quickly.

## 218 Play Testing Keeps Going [Team Management]
- Experienced playtesters can spend far more time on competitive balance than the developers. Use their analysis to extend what the team can cover.
- The director watches for tuning that would erase a fighter’s identity. Divergent opinions can mean the characters offer distinct strengths.
- Online updates make it possible to improve balance after launch using evidence such as tournament results.

## 232 Directors and Producers [Team Management]
- The director usually owns decisions about the game’s content. Make it clear who decides when the team disagrees.
- The producer handles budget, sales expectations, staffing, publicity, and cost effectiveness from the company’s perspective, often across several projects.
- Division of labor varies and one person may hold both roles. Keep final decision authority explicit.

## 246 Elementary School Play Testers [Team Management]
- Observe children playing a development build in a relaxed setting. Watch where they respond, get stuck, or lose interest.
- Children’s reactions can be more direct and can lift team morale. Run this kind of test when safety and confidentiality can be handled.

## 256 A Director’s Job is to Trim [Team Management]
- Directors decide and adjust course. When a delivery slips or the budget grows, cut costly or risky elements to finish the project.
- Flag a likely miss early with accurate information. A late announcement leaves fewer options for correcting course.
