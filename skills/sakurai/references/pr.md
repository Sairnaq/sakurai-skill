# Marketing (showing gameplay, setting expectations, demos, updates)

Summaries based on the Japanese captions for Masahiro Sakurai on Creating Games. Episode numbers follow the repository’s oldest-first source order.

## 080 Marketing is Multiplicative [Marketing]
- Development and marketing multiply each other’s impact. Developers can help players discover the work.
- Steam was estimated to see over 10,000 releases in 2021. A game needs to earn attention in a busy market.
- Build assets such as fighter reveals for use in the game and as standalone promotions.
- Excessive announcements can annoy readers. Developers can review planned announcements and live-service updates and offer feedback.

## 101 Paint an Accurate Picture of Your Game [Marketing]
- Match the title, packaging, screenshots, and previews to what players get. Misleading materials miss the audience that would like the game and disappoint people who expected something else.
- Save surprises for the game. At the storefront, use art to communicate the appeal and make players want to pick it up.
- Think about the title’s short form. In Japanese, consider whether it has a memorable four-character abbreviation, like Smash Bros.
- Kirby Super Star’s Japanese packaging used a simple paulownia-box design to convey the spirit of the product indirectly.

## 121 Game Demos [Marketing]
- A demo can introduce people to the game and take as much work as a small standalone release.
- A prerelease demo requires removing spoilers from logs and files, maintaining separate builds and updates, and testing at release quality. Poor tuning can hurt the game’s reputation.
- Building a demo from a finished game is easier, though it serves promotion later. A tuned demo may arrive after release, and online distribution can help it reach players.

## 141 Official Websites [Marketing]
- Use the official site for comprehensive and accurate information, and social posts for short updates.
- For a large game, organize features, instructions, stages, music, and videos into a clear hierarchy.
- Some people assumed the original Smash Bros. was shallow based on appearances. The team published instructions and strategy material before release to give players a clearer picture.
- If players have trouble seeing the appeal, provide a guide that explains how to play and where the fun comes from.

## 161 Fighter Reveal Videos [Marketing]
- In-game movies can circulate online before launch. The team designed 37 self-contained reveal videos that could entertain viewers at any point.
- The director plans the shots, gestures, and musical timing, then works with the production company on storyboards, animatics, CG, and sound. Gameplay is captured through replay and a debug camera.
- Review each stage. Common adjustments include posture, layout, interactions with other characters, the balance between comedy and seriousness, and synchronization to the music.

## 184 Show the Actual Game! [Marketing]
- Players use gameplay footage to decide whether to buy. Give them a chance to see the game in action.
- Show gameplay early in the trailer, including the main storefront video. Keep logos, intros, character introductions, and setting exposition brief.
- New properties and indie games especially benefit from opening with gameplay. Established franchises and creators can sometimes lead with a teaser.
- Keep a promo brisk and show the appeal at a glance.
- A tech demo can use more staging when it highlights a real capability of the game’s systems.

## 205 Playing Games in Front of Other People [Marketing]
- A live demonstration can be the strongest presentation. Cover signature moves and highlights while avoiding a one-sided match or too much repetition of standard moves.
- Preparing a demo requires understanding the game. Planning what to show can help clarify your thinking.
- Rehearse the gameplay in your head and imagine the audience’s reactions to spot problems.

## 224 Targeting and Market Research [Marketing]
- Market data describes existing products. Following it too closely can lead to crowded territory, such as the many RPGs modeled on Dragon Quest.
- Players do not fit neatly into age or gender groups. The original Kirby was designed for beginners of any age.
- Use research when a commercial decision needs it. Give original ideas room to develop beyond existing market categories.

## 243 Smash Bros. DOJO!! [Marketing]
- Build awareness alongside development. If a game’s depth is hard to see, use the official site for instructions and strategies.
- The DOJO!! published development images and updates and responded to comments. One update every weekday, in several languages, gave readers something to look forward to.
- The director is well placed to explain the game. If someone else writes the updates, set aside time to review them.
